Plan capacity is metered in **security runs**. The console shows the meter as
"This scan will use N of your remaining monthly security runs"; plan material
sometimes calls the same units *usage credits*, and the API carries the number
a scan was launched with as `security_run_units`. Whatever the label, one run
is not one scan: a full assessment usually charges four, a focused review
charges one, and a failed run charges nothing.

That is not a rounding detail, it is the pricing rule. This page covers the two
numbers behind every scan — what it reserves and what it is charged — which
pipeline work is worth a unit, and what happens to the unit when a scan does
not finish.

## Two numbers, not one

Every scan writes one row to the account's run ledger at launch, in the same
transaction that creates the scan, and that row is amended once when the scan
reaches a terminal state.

- **Reservation** — the ceiling. Five for a full assessment, one for a focused
  review. This is what the monthly allowance sees while the scan is in flight.
- **Charge** — what the ledger settles to when the scan finishes: zero, one per
  completed stage, or the reservation itself when the outcome cannot be priced
  from stage records.

The reservation exists because settlement happens after the fact. If the cap
counted only finished work, two full assessments could launch against the same
remaining balance and both pass the check; reserving the ceiling is what makes
the allowance hold under concurrency. It is also why the notice above the
launch button states what the scan *will use* rather than a precise forecast: a
scan is not priced until it has run.

On a plan with no monthly allowance (Enterprise in the current catalog) the cap
never trips, but scans still write ledger rows, so the month stays readable if
the account moves to a plan with a limit.

| While the scan is | The ledger holds | The monthly allowance counts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| queued, running, awaiting batch | the reservation | the reservation |
| finished | the settled charge | the settled charge |

## A unit per stage that finished

Settlement is derived from the scan's own stage records: one run for each
pipeline stage that reached successful completion. A stage that raised is
recorded as failed and earns nothing.

| Stage | Runs when | What it delivers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coordinator | every assessment | The research loop: reads, probes, dispatches workers |
| Rechecks | retest scope, or verification-only | Re-tests of previous findings |
| Triage | every assessment | Severity, confidence, dedup, the finding set |
| HackerOne eligibility review | the scan opted in | Bounty eligibility per finding |
| Adversarial review | the scan opted in (on by default) | Vendor-style rebuttal pass |
| Artifacts | every assessment | PoC, disclosure email, deployment package |

The deterministic passes — SAST, SCA, infrastructure and secret inventory,
fingerprint refresh, confidence recompute — are not stages and cost no runs.
They consume no agent budget and produce nothing a unit pays for, and counting
them would add a fixed cost to every scan.

A default full source, binary, or remote assessment completes four stages:
coordinator, triage, adversarial review, artifacts.

## What a scan settles at

| Scan shape | Reserves | Settles at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full source, binary, or remote assessment | 5 | 4 runs (four completed stages) |
| … with HackerOne eligibility review | 5 | 5 |
| … with retest scope ("re-check previous findings") | 5 | 5, or 6 with HackerOne review too |
| Changed-file review (focused rescan, CI pull-request review) | 1 | 1, however many stages it walked |
| Verification-only retest | 1 | 1 |
| Failed | 5 or 1 | 0 |
| Completed but stalled, or authentication degraded mid-run | 5 or 1 | 0 |
| Cancelled | 5 or 1 | one per stage finished before the cancel |
| Any of the above with no stage records | 5 or 1 | the reservation stands |

The focused cap and the refund rule need more than a table row.

### Focused products are capped at one

A changed-file review runs the whole spine — coordinator, triage, adversarial
review, artifacts — scoped to the diff. Priced per stage it would cost the same
as a repository sweep, so the charge is capped at one run however much of that
spine it walks. A verification-only retest reaches one on its own: it runs the
rechecks stage and returns.

These are the entry-point products. A three-file pull-request review should not
cost what a whole repository assessment costs, or nobody would run one on every
pull request.

### A run that delivered nothing is free

A failed scan settles to zero. So does a scan that reached `completed` without
delivering the assessment: the coordinator's no-progress guard stopped it early,
or authentication degraded mid-run and the findings cannot be trusted. Both are
recorded and surfaced on the report; neither is charged.

Healthy interruptions are not in this class. A runner dropout, a control-plane
restart, or an automatic retry is a scan that still completed, and it stays
billable.

Making failure free is deliberate: a retry is the normal recovery path, and
charging for it would make a flaky target or a bad credential expensive to
diagnose. The bound on repeated failures is the concurrency limit, not the run
allowance. The tokens such a run burned are still real, and still appear in
model usage — see [Model usage is a different meter](#model-usage-is-a-different-meter).

## Worked examples

1. **Default full source scan.** Reserves 5. Coordinator, triage, adversarial
   review, and artifacts complete; HackerOne review is off and there is no retest
   scope. Settles at 4. The month shows 4 used, not 5.
2. **The same scan with HackerOne eligibility review.** Five stages finish.
   Settles at 5.
3. **HackerOne review plus re-check previous findings.** Six stages. Settles at
   6 — one over the launch reservation, deliberately: reserving the true maximum
   would let a single launch consume a small plan's whole allowance.
4. **Changed-file review of a three-file pull request.** Reserves 1 and settles
   1, through the same spine as example 1.
5. **Verification-only retest of two baseline findings.** Reserves 1, runs only
   rechecks, settles 1.
6. **Cancelled after the coordinator.** Settles 1: the coordinator's work was
   delivered. The per-source allowance is untouched.
7. **Failed during triage.** Settles 0 on both ledgers.

## When the charge is the reservation

A scan with no stage records at all is priced at its reservation, not at zero.
That covers a worker on an isolated plane whose stage rows never shipped back, a
scan that predates stage metering, and a scan cancelled before its worker wrote
anything. Charging those as zero-stage runs would hand out free scans on the
path that is hardest to audit, so the fallback takes the conservative side.

If a scan you cancelled while it was still queued holds its reservation, this is
why: there is no stage record to price, and the same absent-record path protects
the scans that really did run.

## The ledger, the month, and deleting a scan

- The month is the UTC calendar month the scan was **launched** in. Settlement
  amends the row's units, not its date, so a scan launched on the last day of
  the month and settled the next day charges the launch month.
- The allowance resets at the start of each month. Nothing rolls over.
- The allowance belongs to the account, not to the member: every scan any member
  launches draws on the same monthly balance.
- Ledger rows outlive the scan they describe. Deleting a finished scan does not
  return its runs; otherwise an account at its cap would refill by deleting its
  history.
- A scan can only be deleted after it finishes. Cancel it first — deleting an
  in-flight scan would strand its reservation with nothing left to adjudicate.
- The check happens twice: a preflight when you submit, so the refusal names
  your real usage instead of the launch dying later, and a transactional check
  in the same transaction that writes the scan, so two launches racing each
  other cannot both slip past. Over the allowance, the launch is refused with a
  402 and nothing is charged.

## Per-source allowance on free and trial plans

Free and trial plans bound a second quantity: how much a single source can
consume per month, across every account that scans it. The source is the
repository owner (`github.com/acme`) for a git target, or the hostname for a
remote target. The allowance is shared, and it survives account closure, so
signing up again does not reset it.

A completed billable run burns its source allowance. A failed or cancelled run
does not. Paid plans have no per-source limit. When the gate trips, the message
names the source and shows its shared usage next to your own.

## Model usage is a different meter

Security runs measure plan capacity. They are not a proxy for token spend, and
the two are metered separately:

- Every LLM call records input, cache-hit, and output tokens against the scan
  and its project, which is what the per-scan usage breakdown reports.
- Hosted-model spend is invoiced monthly per model. Calls made with your own
  provider key are billed by that provider and are not invoiced by ZeroQuarry.
- Caps on monthly tokens and on platform-funded dollars are enforced per call,
  independently of the run allowance. A model funded by your own key bypasses
  those caps, because the spend is not ours.
- Settlement of a run unit never touches token records. A refunded run still
  burned tokens, and those tokens still appear in usage.

## Where to read the number

- **Account > Billing and Usage** — "N of M security runs used" for the current
  month, against the plan's allowance. In-flight scans count at their
  reservation.
- **The launch form** — the notice above the submit button states what this scan
  reserves from the remaining allowance, and says so up front when the allowance
  cannot fit it.
- **The 402 page** — current usage, the source's shared usage where relevant,
  and the remedy that matches your role: upgrade yourself, or ask the account
  owner.
- **The API** — every scan and report object carries `security_run_units`, the
  number the scan reserved at launch. Per-scan token spend is in the report's
  usage breakdown.

## Why it is shaped this way

Billing follows delivered value rather than launches. A scan that failed, that
you cancelled, or that never authenticated did not give you an assessment, so
it should not cost a run. A scan that finished three stages gave you three
stages' worth.

The reservation is the counterweight. Settlement is retroactive, and an
allowance that only counted settled work would be trivially overshot by
concurrent launches. Reserving the ceiling and refunding the difference keeps
the cap honest without charging for work that never landed.

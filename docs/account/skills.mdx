ZeroQuarry serves a pack of skills to AI agents that connect over
[MCP](/workflows/mcp). A skill is a document that teaches an agent how to do
one job well — what to confirm before launching a scan, how to record a
verdict, when a dependency is safe to add.

**Account > Skills** is where you control which of them your agents get.

## The console

The Skills page is a list of the skills available to your account, each with
a switch. Turning one off means agents connected to your account stop
receiving it. Turning one on restores it.

Two things sit above the list:

- **Your resolved policy** — what your account has settled on, and whether
  that came from an explicit choice or from the pack default.
- **Recent activity** — the last 20 changes made to the policy, who made
  them, and when.

Each skill in the list carries its provenance: the template version it was
rendered from, who approved it, the digests of its files, and the version of
the pack it came from. That is what lets you tell a skill that has been
updated apart from one that has not.

## Who can manage skills

Everything on this page needs the **manage skills** permission, which belongs
to the **owner** and **admin** roles. Members and the billing role do not
have it.

The curation queue is under the same gate.

## How the policy decides

Each account has a policy for each skill. The rule is simple:

1. **An explicit choice wins.** If you have set this skill to on or off, that
   is the answer.
2. **Otherwise the pack default applies.** A skill ships enabled if it is
   part of the pack, and disabled if it is not.

One skill is exempt: the **entrypoint** skill is always served and cannot be
switched off. It is what the server's own discovery instructions point an
agent at, and an agent that cannot reach it cannot route itself to anything
else.

## Where the policy applies

The policy is enforced on **both** MCP surfaces. A client that reads the pack
through the native skills surface gets the filtered pack, and a client that
falls back to the `list_skills` and `load_skill` tools gets the same filtered
pack. A skill switched off in the console is not reachable by either route.

The filtered pack is cached per account with a private cache scope, so one
account's policy never leaks into another's.

## Skill templates

Some skills are not fixed documents. They are **templates**: a document with
slots in it that get filled in from your account's settings, then rendered
into a skill.

Four templates ship today:

| Template | What it governs |
| --- | --- |
| `sdlc-issues-prs` | Work is tracked as issues and changes land through pull requests. Governs how security action items ride your issue-and-PR flow, including where tickets are filed. |
| `sdlc-conventional-commits` | Commit messages follow Conventional Commits and history doubles as the audit trail. Governs how security fixes are named, scoped, and tied to work items. |
| `compliance-soc2` | Maps ZeroQuarry artifacts — scans, findings, verdicts, reports — to the Trust Services Criteria they support, and assembles the evidence summaries you hand over. |
| `compliance-iso27001` | The same mapping for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A controls. |

A rendered skill takes the template's name. The console records which
template version it was rendered from, who approved it, and its digests.

### Choosing a style

The SDLC templates are a choice, not a pair you enable independently. Pick
one **SDLC style**:

- **Issues and pull requests**
- **Conventional commits**

Selecting one stages the matching skill. Clearing it retires the skill.

### The ticketing slot

The SDLC templates take a **ticketing** slot — where a ticket is filed when
a finding needs an owner:

`jira` · `linear` · `servicenow` · `github_issues` · `none`

Setting it restages the style skill atomically, so the skill and its slot
never disagree.

### Staged bytes

A rendered template is **staged**: its bytes are frozen at the moment they
are rendered and versioned with their digests, and the served skill is that
staged version — not a fresh render on every request. That is what makes the
provenance meaningful.

When the template itself is updated, the console tells you that a newer
version is available, and you restage deliberately. A staged skill that has
drifted from the account's current profile is flagged too.

A render that fails validation is excluded rather than served in part, and
the reason is recorded.

## The curation agent

Alongside the manual switches, an AI agent can propose changes to your
policy. It lives under **Account > Skills > Curation**.

**The agent proposes. A person decides.** It has no write authority over
your account: a proposal does nothing until somebody approves it. This is
deliberate, not a limitation — the agent is there to notice patterns you
would have to click through yourself, not to reconfigure your account.

The queue shows each proposal with its rationale, and lets you approve,
reject, or dismiss it. You can filter by status and page through the
history, and the page reports how many proposals have been accepted and the
median time to approval.

### What the agent is allowed to see

Only counts and profile metadata: your tier, and aggregate scan counts. It
never reads finding content, repository content, or scan results. A proposal
it writes is therefore about the shape of your usage — "you run a lot of
binary scans" — and never about what your findings say.

### What a proposal can do

Each proposal item is one of:

- enable a built-in skill
- disable a skill
- enable a template

The proposal is validated before it is shown. If any item in it is invalid,
the run fails closed and the rejected items are reported — a partially valid
proposal is not offered as if it were fully fine.

Only one curation run is in flight at a time. A run that gets stuck is
reclaimed after 15 minutes rather than blocking the queue forever.

## What is recorded

Every change to the policy is written to an administrative audit trail: the
skill, who changed it, when, and what changed. A proposal that is generated,
approved, applied, rejected, or dismissed is recorded there too.

A change that cannot be written to the trail does not happen. The mutation
fails rather than proceeding unrecorded.

Many targets gate registration behind a confirmation e-mail, and a magic-link
login puts the working session behind a link in that inbox. An operator watching
a scan would have to notice the prompt, click the link, and hand the session over.

Scan mail removes that step. A scan allocates its own disposable mailbox, uses
the address to register on the target, follows the magic link, and collects the
session — without anyone watching.

## How it works

1. The agent allocates a mailbox for the scan.
2. It submits that address in the target's registration flow.
3. The target sends mail to it.
4. The agent polls for the message and follows the link.
5. The click lands the session cookie in the scan's shared cookie jar
   automatically.

That last step is why this works without an operator. The agent is not
hand-parsing cookies out of a URL; the link click goes through the same request
path as any other, and the resulting cookie lands where the rest of the scan's
authentication already lives.

## Two things to understand before you use it

**The address is a credential.** Anyone who has it can read the target's mail
sent to that address, and for the life of the scan that includes whatever the
target chooses to send: verification codes, password resets, notifications. It
exists for one scan and is disposed of when the scan reaches a terminal state.

**The mail body is target-controlled input.** A target that can send mail to an
address the agent reads has a channel for putting text in front of the agent. The
tools say so in their output, explicitly, because this is the kind of thing that
is easy to forget and expensive to get wrong. Mail content is data to be
examined, never instructions to be followed.

## Requirements

Mailbox allocation needs both:

- the platform mail poller configured
- the account tier to carry incoming e-mail triage

Without them the tools refuse rather than half-working. There is no partial
state where a mailbox silently receives nothing.

## Where the mail lives

Stored mail is per scan and belongs to that scan. It follows the account's
retention policy, and the mailbox is disposed of at every terminal scan state
rather than only on success — a cancelled or failed scan does not leave a
readable mailbox behind.

On split-plane workers, allocation and reads go through the control plane. A
mailbox minted only in a worker's local database would never receive routed
mail, and the stored mail lives in the control-plane database anyway.

## Retention and your data-handling posture

Per-scan inboxes generate target-visible e-mail traffic, and their contents are
data your account is holding. That is worth weighing before enabling it:

- what the target will send to a throwaway address
- how long stored mail is kept before the retention sweep
- whether your own policy already restricts inbound e-mail triage

See [Data handling](/security/data-handling) for how this sits with the rest of
what ZeroQuarry stores.

An attestation engagement hands a point-in-time finding set to a **third
party** — an external assessor, pentest firm, or security team using their own
ZeroQuarry account — and captures what they conclude about it, finding by
finding.

The result is evidence rather than another self-issued report. Every verdict and
the sealed manifest record *who* attested, and for which firm: the attestor's
identity is deliberately distinct from the customer's, and that separation is
the whole point of the artifact.

## When to use an engagement

- A customer requires **third-party validation** before they accept the report.
- A pentest-style engagement needs per-finding confirmation against your internal
  scan results.
- A regulator wants **periodic re-attestation** that previously reported findings
  remain fixed.
- You are the attestor: a firm reviewing a client's baseline inside your own
  account.

## Two kinds, one set of machinery

| Kind | Verdicts | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finding confirmation | Confirmed, Rejected, Severity adjusted, Unable to verify | First-pass verification of a scan's findings |
| Remediation verification | Fixed, Partially fixed, Not fixed, Unable to verify, Accepted risk | Re-verifying that reported findings are fixed, anchored to the same baseline |

Everything else is shared. Remediation verification deliberately uses the same
outcome vocabulary as [verification tests](/reports/verification-tests), so a
finding that was closed internally can be confirmed independently without
learning a second set of words.

## How an engagement works

1. **Issue the baseline.** Open the completed report and designate it as evidence
   in the [Evidence Room](/reports/evidence-room). The snapshot freezes the
   finding set, targets, and report context, and the engagement stays anchored to
   that snapshot's integrity hash for its whole life.
2. **Create the engagement.** Start one from the baseline in the Evidence Room
   and choose the kind. You can attach a note for the attestor explaining what you
   need, and pre-authorize credentials they will need to reach their own targets.
3. **Invite the attestor.** Enter their email address. If they already belong to
   an attestor-enabled account they are attached directly; otherwise they receive
   a one-time invitation to accept while signed in to their own account.
4. **The attestor works.** From their own account, the attestor reviews each
   baseline finding with its evidence, records a verdict and optional note, runs
   their own retests, and may add findings of their own.
5. **They shape the working document.** Before signing, the attestor decides what
   the deliverable actually says.
6. **Sign-off seals the record.** Signing seals a canonical manifest and two PDF
   variants. Both sides can then download and cite them.

Sign-off is gated on every finding having a verdict. Review notes are optional
attestor commentary and never block it, so an attestor is not forced to write an
essay per finding to get paid.

## The attestor's side

The attestor works inside their **own** ZeroQuarry account, against their own
workspace. They see the engagement and its baseline, not the rest of your
workspace.

Because they are running real assessments, the engagement carries a **warrant**:
the set of hosts the baseline actually covers, taken from your project's URL and
host assets plus what the source scan pointed at. The workspace scan form shows
the warrant and flags a target that falls outside it, so an attestor can see the
authorization boundary rather than infer it.

Only **open** engagements grant a warrant. Once an engagement is attested or
cancelled it is history, not live authorization.

## Findings the attestor adds

A finding confirmation used to be a review exercise: the attestor could confirm,
reject, or adjust what you already had. It is not only that any more. The attestor
can add findings they discovered that were not on your baseline at all.

Each added finding is a full finding with its own type, severity, CVSS vector,
description, impact, remediation, and evidence. It also carries provenance — where
it came from (`retest_scan`, `manual`, or `imported`), which scan or finding it
originated in, and who added it by name and email. That provenance line travels
with it into the working document.

Added findings are not a side channel. They go through the same lifecycle as
baseline findings: they can be retested, they can be accepted or rejected in a
later round, and they land inside the sealed manifest.

## The working document

Sealing a verdict record would produce a defensible but mechanical artifact. The
attestor can instead shape the document that goes out, on the export screen:

- **Drop** a baseline finding the working document should not carry
- **Adjust** a severity without rewriting the verdict record
- **Add** findings of their own

Findings the attestor rejected are omitted by default, so the common case needs no
editing. A dropped finding is remembered by name so the export screen can show
what was removed, and a malformed severity override is rejected rather than
silently published as a mangled report.

Anything included this way carries a provenance line naming the attestor who added
it. Dropping a finding from the document does not remove it from the sealed
record — the manifest still holds the complete verdict history, which is what
makes the artifact provable.

## Sign-off seals two documents, not one

This is the part worth understanding, because it is why releasing and sealing are
separate acts.

At sign-off the attestor seals **both** the full report and its counts-only
sibling. Sealing both up front is deliberate: a PDF rendered later would not be
the bytes the manifest hash covers, so a later release of the full document would
be unverifiable. Sealing both makes the whole ladder provable from the start.

The sealed PDF is either **generated** from the verdict record, or the attestor's
**own edited document** converted to PDF, with that source document's hash
retained for provenance. When a firm signs a deliverable it has produced itself,
the sealed bytes are the firm's document, and the record says so.

The manifest is canonical — serialized with sorted keys — so its hash is stable,
and both PDF variants are cited by name, size, hash, source and detail. A holder of
either document can prove which sealed artifact it is.

## Releasing is separate from sealing

Sealing is final. Releasing controls what the **requestor** can currently fetch,
and the attestor can change it afterwards without touching the sealed record.

| Release level | What the requestor gets |
| --- | --- |
| Counts only | Counts and definitions, no per-finding detail |
| Full findings | The complete report |
| Full findings and counts | Both |

An engagement sealed before release tracking existed defaults to the full report,
and deliberately so: withholding it retroactively would break a signature the
requestor already holds. Each release is recorded with the note explaining why it
was made.

## The manifest

The manifest binds the engagement to its baseline: it records the verdict for every
finding, the attestor's identity and firm, the request-time note, the sealed
documents, and the release history. Both parties download it alongside the PDFs,
and it is what an auditor checks when they want to know who attested what, and
against which evidence.

The current manifest schema is version 3. Older manifests remain readable; the
document block always describes the full variant so a v2-era reader still parses
it correctly.

## Both sides keep their boundaries

Engagement ids, verdicts, and artifacts are scoped so neither side can reach the
other's workspace data; a cross-account request resolves as not found.

The attestor's role inside their *own* account is irrelevant to the engagement.
What matters is that their account is enabled for attestation.

An explicit membership row takes precedence over simply being in the same account.
That matters when a firm uses a shared ZeroQuarry workspace: a colleague invited
onto the engagement as the attestor exercises the **attestor** role on it, not the
customer role, even though both sit in the same account.

## Requirements

Engagements require the attestation capability on the customer account and the
attestation side-capability on the attestor's account. If you do not see the
Attestations area, ask ZeroQuarry support to enable it on your plan.

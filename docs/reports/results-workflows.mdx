A finished scan produces findings, and a finding is only useful once somebody
does something with it. The report page is built around two pieces: a strip
that shows where the scan is in the chain of work, and a picker that offers
the next step for whichever goal you have.

## The evidence chain

Across the top of the report runs a four-stage strip:

**Decide → Freeze → Verify → Prove**

Each stage has a state, a short note, and the action that moves it forward.
The strip's headline is the first stage that is still open, so the report
always answers "what is the next thing to do with this scan?".

| Stage | What it means | What moves it forward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Decide** | The findings have not been adjudicated. This is the findings tab's decision queue. | Review the findings and record a verdict on each. |
| **Freeze** | The scan's evidence snapshot — the sealed record of what the scan found. | **Designate as evidence** creates the snapshot. |
| **Verify** | Whether the findings still hold. | A re-check or retest, or a verification test against the frozen evidence. |
| **Prove** | The evidence has been attested to by a third party. | An [attestation engagement](/reports/attestations). |

The stages are a pipeline, not a checklist: freezing before deciding seals
the findings as they are, verification before freezing has nothing sealed to
check, and proving needs the freeze.

Freezing is irreversible. There is no un-designate, which is why the picker
card for it always links to a walk-through rather than posting a freeze from a
single click.

## Work with these results

The **Work with these results** picker on the report header is the way into
the workflows below. It asks what you are trying to do rather than where you
are in the UI, and opens the matching wizard.

The Download menu that used to sit on the report header is folded into this
picker. There is one entry point, not two.

### Share

Two different jobs, two different deliverables. The share wizard asks which
one you mean before it asks anything else.

- **A report for a person** — a document. A human reads it.
- **Share links to specific findings** — links. A human opens them in the
  console.

That distinction runs through the whole wizard: what goes in it, what the
recipient receives, and how long it lives are all asked in the light of the
answer to the first question.

The wizard is four steps: **Who is this for? → What's in it? → Delivery →
Review & confirm**. Which findings it covers can be narrowed by selection and
by the filters active on the report, and the review step says plainly how
many findings are included.

See [Secure sharing](/reports/secure-sharing) for how share links work, and
[Exports and disclosures](/reports/exports-and-disclosures) for the other way
results leave ZeroQuarry.

### Send to a tool

For getting results into another tool rather than to a person. The wizard is
three steps: **Where are these results going? → Which file formats? → Review
& confirm**.

The first step is the ingestion point, because it decides the formats. A code
scanner wants SARIF. A dependency-review tool wants an SBOM. Picking the
destination first means the format list is the formats that destination
actually accepts, rather than every format ZeroQuarry can produce.

Everything you pick arrives in one download.

### Verify fixes

For checking whether findings still hold. The wizard is three steps: **Which
findings should be re-verified? → Review the findings → Review & confirm**.

Step 1 sets the scope. The counts are as of right now, and step 2 shows
exactly what will be re-checked, so nothing is discovered by surprise. You
can narrow the scope to a list you pick, or trim the re-checkable candidates
by hand — each candidate is a row with its severity, title, and citation, and
the wizard shows how many are selected out of how many are available.

Step 2 is the review, and it separates the two halves of a re-check. The
**accepted** side is the findings you want checked. The **rejected** side is
the findings you are withdrawing from the re-check, each with a reason. A
finding that adversarial review already ended up rejecting starts unchecked,
so it has to be put back deliberately rather than swept along.

Step 3 confirms what will happen and what it costs.

A verify run re-checks findings against the cited code or the live target.
**Nothing new is discovered** — it is a check on what is already there, not
another scan. For a scan that looks for new problems, see
[Source code scans](/scans/source-code).

### Prove it

For putting a third party behind the result. The card's copy depends on
where the scan is: if the results are already sealed as evidence and an
attestation is running, the card tracks it; if they are sealed but no
attestation is running, it starts one; if they are not sealed, it walks you
through sealing them first.

See [Attestation engagements](/reports/attestations) for the full flow.

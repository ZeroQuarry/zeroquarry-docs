"Scan it again" means three different things in ZeroQuarry, and picking the
wrong one wastes a run. This page names the three, then covers what a re-check
produces and how to read it.

## The three re-run meanings

The report page deliberately names them differently, because they differ in
what they cost and what they can find.

### 1. Re-run stage on selected findings

Re-runs one stage against **the findings you selected**. Use it when you have
fixed something and want to know whether that specific finding is gone.

### 2. Re-run a pipeline stage (whole scan)

Re-runs the chosen stage over the **whole scan, in place**. It discovers
nothing new and creates no new version — it redoes a stage of the scan you
already have.

### 3. Scan again — new version

Runs a **full discovery pass** against the target again, and may find new
issues beyond the findings this scan already found. This creates a new
version of the scan. On a remote scan the re-check runs alongside it by
default, and unchecking it skips the retest; either way it reserves security
runs like any other scan.

If you are not sure which you want, it is almost always one of these:

- **I fixed something, is it gone?** → Re-run stage on selected findings.
- **I do not trust that stage, run it again for everything.** → Re-run a
  pipeline stage (whole scan).
- **I changed the code, what else is wrong now?** → Scan again — new version.

## Re-checking carries your selection and filters

The re-check flow is goal-first: you choose the scope, and the findings
already selected on the report, together with the filters you have applied,
carry through to the re-check. You do not have to rebuild the set by hand.

The re-checkable candidates can also be trimmed from a list — see
[Verify fixes](/reports/results-workflows#verify-fixes) in the results picker
page.

## What a re-check produces

### Retest results are separate from new findings

A retest reports on the findings it checked. It does not mix them in with
the scan's finding list, because they answer different questions. The report
shows them as **Retest results**, and the new findings from the same scan as
**New findings from this scan**.

When a retest re-confirms an occurrence of a finding that was already known,
that is counted as a re-confirmation rather than as a new finding.

### Outcomes

| Outcome | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed | The finding is gone. |
| Still present | The finding is still there. |
| Partially fixed | Some of it is gone and some is not. The residual severity is carried through. |
| Inconclusive | The retest could not decide. |

A partial remediation is recorded as a **partially fixed** verdict rather
than being forced into fixed or still-present. Without a residual severity it
degrades to inconclusive, because a partial fix that nobody has quantified
is not a partial fix you can report.

## A blocked retest is not a failure

Sometimes a re-check cannot run because the session it needed is gone — the
login expired, the credentials stopped working, or the target is
unreachable. That is a **blocked** retest, and it is reported honestly
rather than being dressed up as a result.

| Blocked reason | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| Session expired | The authenticated session had expired. |
| Credentials invalid | The configured login no longer works. |
| Target unreachable | The target could not be reached. |

A blocked retest consumes **zero security runs**. You are told which reason
applies and what to do about it — for an expired session, run a fresh retest
with a fresh session.

The console distinguishes three states that are easy to conflate:

- **BLOCKED** — the retest was blocked outright.
- **CREDENTIAL HEALTH UNKNOWN** — static credentials are in use and their
  health could not be established.
- **DEGRADED** — the session tracked or canary died partway through, so the
  result is weaker than a clean run.

## The per-finding retest timeline

Each finding carries its own **Retest history** panel, next to its decision
history. It shows the outcome badges, where each run came from, and the
reasoning recorded for each result.

The retest evidence lives here rather than in an accordion on the report
page, so the history of a finding and the finding's decisions sit together.

## Rechecks across runs in an engagement

When an attestor re-checks findings across more than one scan, and two
completed scans disagree about the same finding, the engagement surfaces the
disagreement rather than picking a winner. Both outcomes are shown side by
side, with the scan and its date.

A targeted retest can also be **preflighted** before it is queued: the
engagement shows how many findings would be checked, what is blocking them,
which targets are involved, and any model, rate-limit, or scope overrides
that would apply.
